Business Analytics in the Age of AI

LLMs, prompts, and agents: using AI to accelerate analysis without outsourcing analytical judgment.

Focus: AI literacy + business analysisFormat: concepts, demos, discussionDuration: 3.5 hours
Course recap

AI adds a new layer to the workflow.

Business question→SQL and data→Dashboard→Decision→AI-assisted workflow
Framing: AI can accelerate the process, but the analyst remains responsible for definitions, verification, interpretation, and the decision.
1 · What is AI?

AI is a family of capabilities, not one tool.

Artificial intelligence

Performs intelligent tasks

Systems that perceive, reason, generate, recommend, or act.

Machine learning

Learns patterns from data

Predicts, classifies, ranks, or detects patterns.

Generative AI

Creates new content

Text, code, images, audio, and structured outputs.

Keep the taxonomy simple: an LLM is one type of generative AI; an agent is a workflow that uses a model and tools.

2 · What is an LLM?

An LLM predicts language in context.

It is not a database, not automatically a search engine, and not a guaranteed source of truth.

RevenuebycategoryinQ3increasedbecause...
Core idea: the model generates one token at a time based on patterns learned during training and the context supplied in the conversation.
2 · What is an LLM?

Five ideas students need.

Tokens

Pieces of text

Words, parts of words, punctuation, and symbols processed by the model.

Context

Working information

The model can only reason from the context it can see.

Embeddings

Numerical meaning

Representations that capture relationships among concepts.

Variation

Predictability

Settings can make outputs more deterministic or diverse.

Hallucination

Plausible error

Confident-looking content can still be unsupported or wrong.

Verification

Analyst responsibility

Check calculations, sources, definitions, and assumptions.

3 · Neural networks

High-level: learned transformations.

Input: tokens and context
Layers: numerical transformations
Attention: what context matters now?
Output: next-token probabilities
No equations required: training adjusts weights so the network becomes better at recognizing patterns and producing useful predictions. The connections represent learned numerical transformations, not human-like thoughts.

4 · AI for business analysis

AI can support every stage of the workflow.

Frame

Clarify the question

Turn “sales are down” into measurable questions, metrics, dimensions, and time periods.

Prepare

Understand the data

Explain columns, suggest quality checks, and document a data dictionary.

Analyze

Draft SQL and DAX

Generate query ideas, explain joins, debug formulas, and propose validation checks.

Communicate

Interpret and explain

Draft summaries, alternative explanations, follow-up questions, and stakeholder versions.

4 · AI for business analysis

Use AI as an analytical collaborator, not an oracle.

Risky request
“Analyze this data and tell me what to do.”

Missing definitions, context, grain, validation, and audience.

Better request
“Using the order schema, identify three measurable explanations for declining revenue. State the tables, fields, grain, query plan, and validation check. Do not invent results.”

Specific task, context, constraints, and quality criteria.

6 · Prompt engineering

A strong prompt is clear analytical communication.

Role+Task+Context+Constraints+Output+Checks
Act as a business analyst.
Context: use the attached online-store schema.
Task: propose three questions about declining revenue.
Constraints: state grain; do not invent results.
Output: return a table with question, metric, fields, and validation step.
6 · Prompt engineering

How models interpret our prompts.

Context accumulates

Earlier instructions and examples affect later output.

Ambiguity survives

“Sales,” “customer,” or “top” need operational definitions.

Examples anchor format

Show the structure and level of detail you expect.

Constraints reduce drift

Tell the model what not to assume or fabricate.

Long context can conflict

Separate instructions, data, examples, and questions.

Ask for uncertainty

Require assumptions, evidence, and follow-up checks.

7 · Skills, tools, and agents

From one prompt to a workflow.

Prompt: one request, one response
Skill: repeatable capability with defined output
Tool: read, query, execute, search, create
Agent: plan → act → inspect → revise
Example skill: dashboard accessibility checker. It receives a report description, checks labels, contrast, chart choice, and filter clarity, then returns prioritized changes.
7 · Agentic AI

Agents increase capability—and responsibility.

Goal→Inspect schema→Run queries→Check results→Draft brief→Human review
Useful autonomy

Multi-step investigation

Find possible reasons for a revenue decline, validate comparisons, and prepare evidence.

Required controls

Permissions and review

Limit file and database access, log actions, define success criteria, and approve outputs.

More autonomy means more risk: never allow an agent to make an irreversible business decision without human review.
8 · Integrated activity

Design an AI-assisted analysis workflow.

  1. Choose one online-store business question.
  2. Identify the required tables, fields, metric, and grain.
  3. Write the prompt you would use.
  4. Choose browser LLM, coding assistant, or agent—and explain why.
  5. Define the validation check before trusting the result.
  6. State one privacy, reliability, or interpretation risk.
Peer challenge: Can another student reproduce your intended workflow from your instructions?
9 · Final check

Four questions before acting on AI output.

Calculation

Is the result numerically correct?

Grain

Are rows, joins, and aggregations aligned?

Interpretation

Does the evidence support the claim?

Decision

What must be verified before acting?

Course principle: AI can accelerate business analysis, but it does not remove the need for analytical thinking.
Close

Frame the question. Verify the output. Own the decision.

Frame→Provide context→Use the right tool→Verify→Communicate→Decide

The future analyst is not the person who asks AI to do everything. It is the person who knows what to ask, how to check it, and when not to trust it.
